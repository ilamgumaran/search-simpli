## Source

**Why.** J2 measured two losses that share one cause: the engine has no way to weigh a file's own name against its body.

1. **Title intent under `content`.** A child who asks for "my science homework" names the file. Under `content` (what `search_knowledge` does by default, and the app's first query since M16-T21), the title slice is 0.000 success@1 and 0.258 MRR@10 on both surfaces. Denser bodies win, and two files whose words are only in their name are not in the top 10 at all (`ti08`, `ti10`).
2. **`both` overrates one name word.** `both` lifts the title slice to 0.545, but it costs control three rank-1 answers: `c02` (`pe-kit.txt`), `c06` (`emails-to-school.md`, rank 4) and `c07` (`fish-tank.md`). Control under `both` is 0.870. In all three, a one-word stem match is fused at name rank 1 by equal-weight RRF above the full body answer. The folder-only rows `ti06`, `ti07` and `ti11` stay at ranks 5, 3 and 3, because the file-name channel sees only the stem.

**What changes.** This task adds a small second field per file, `title-field-v1`:

- **The text.** The file's root-relative folder names and its stem, tokenized by the same analyzer-v2 that the file-name channel uses.
- **Where it comes from.** It is derived in memory, the first time an opened generation needs it, from the record paths the documents section already holds. **No section, manifest, writer or format change**, so no phone index is rebuilt.
- **How it scores.** BM25 over the files, multiplied by **coverage**: the share of the query's distinct terms that the field holds. That product, times a fixed weight **w = 2.5**, is added to the lexical score of the file's best-matching body chunk. When no body chunk matches, it goes on the file's first chunk.
- **`content`** gets it in its lexical channel.
- **`both`** becomes a score-level merge: one result per file, scored by its best body chunk plus the same title part. The equal-weight RRF goes.
- **`filename`** is unchanged.

**Measured (the Python reference).** Python 3.9.6, J2 at `8025d9f`. The prototype is `docs/tasks/S5-T2-prototype.md`; its reimplemented baseline equals `baseline.json`'s Python cells query for query.

| | Title slice success@1 · MRR@10 | Control success@1 | `c02`, `c06`, `c07` under `both` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `content` | 0.000 · 0.258 → **0.909 · 0.955** | 1.000 → **1.000** | |
| `both` | 0.545 · 0.685 → **0.909 · 0.955** | 0.870 → **1.000** | 2, 4, 2 → **1, 1, 1** |

Every other slice is within the S5-T1 gate:
- the largest MRR@10 drop is morphology `content`, −0.013;
- the largest worsening of a first grade-2 rank is 1 place;
- overall success@1 rises: `content` 0.421 → 0.526, `both` 0.432 → 0.516.

The research doc's guess was +0.30. The measured gain is +0.909 under `content`, because the coverage factor lets the weight be large without costing control.

**What was rejected, and the numbers that rejected it:**
- **Weighted RRF.** It leaves control under `both` at 0.870 at every stem weight from 0.25 to 0.75. RRF cannot say that a one-word name match is weak.
- **An additive title part without coverage.** It loses `c02` at w 2.0 while title is still 0.727 at w 1.5. There is no safe weight.
- **Stem only.** It loses the three folder rows (title 0.727).
- **A writer-written section.** See ruling 1.

**The cost at 10k** (scratch Zig bench, `ReleaseSmall`, not under the timing gate: load 3.7–6.2):

| Step | Measured | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The derive, once per opened generation | 4.14–4.21 ms p50 | |
| What a reader keeps | about 1.24 MB | |
| The derive's transient bytes | 14.8 MB | `lexical_build` in an arena; ruling 3 forbids that path |
| The per-query title walk | 0.0006 ms with no title term; 0.034 ms when a query term names all 10,000 files | |

The bars below are therefore restated after the builder's profile under the gate, as in S4-T18.

## Rulings

1. **The design, and what it is not.**

   **The choice.** A title field derived at first use from the documents section's record paths. Weighed against it:
   - **(a) A new versioned writer section** (folder + stem postings written by `apply`, `index` and `import-json`, bound by the manifest). Rejected:
     - it needs a manifest flag bit, so a reader before it refuses the generation with `UnsupportedFlags` and the app can never downgrade;
     - it touches every writer and the S4-T13 carry;
     - it still needs the derive for every generation written before it, or else the field appears on a phone only after its next import;
     - all of that would save about 4 ms once per opened generation at 10k.

     It is filed in 03-next as "only if a phone measurement shows the derive matters".
   - **(b) Building the field from the file-name catalog** plus a folder table. Rejected: it exists only for file-name-enabled indexes, and the catalog's own first load costs 3.2 ms at 10k (S4-T18), no cheaper than the derive.
   - **(c) Weighted RRF, or RRF with a folders+stem channel, for `both`.** Rejected on the measured control rows (Source).
   - **(d) BM25F.** Rejected, as the research doc ruled: it is the second field, not a field-weighted term frequency.
   - **(e) The title part spread over every body-matching chunk of the file.** Rejected: the same title numbers, more chunks of one file in the top 10, and morphology `content` 0.326 against 0.349.

2. **The field, exactly (`title-field-v1`).**

   **The record path.** For each file (each distinct record path with at least one body chunk), take the root-relative path:
   - the root is `scope.rootOfPath`;
   - the relative part is `scope.relativePath`;
   - a legacy `r-legacy` path is taken whole;
   - a path under no root is taken whole.

   **The folder names.** Every component but the last. **The root id is never a folder word.** The app's root ids are opaque (`library` and Shared-folder ids), and on J2 the root `school` costs `ti09` at w 3.0.

   **The stem.** The last component without its final `.ext`, where a name whose only dot is its first character keeps the whole name (Python's `Path.stem`). For example, `notes.v2.md` gives `notes.v2`.

   > **Finding, not to be fixed here.** `filename_field.stem` gives `""` for a dot-first name while Python gives the name. The extension filter keeps such files out of today's indexes. The builder adds a test that pins both behaviours, and a 03-next row.

   **The tokens.** The folder names, then the stem, each tokenized with analyzer-v2 (`analyzer_v2.tokenize` in Zig, `_filename_tokenize` in Python: NFC, variation selectors dropped, simple case fold). `swimming-lessons/autumn-term.md` gives `swimming lessons autumn term`. The term frequency counts repeats across folders and stem.

   **The score** of file f for query q, over the files in the request's scope:

   - Q is the distinct analyzer-v2 tokens of q.
   - BM25 is computed with the engine's parameters (k1 1.2, b 0.75; `scoring.bm25Contribution` in Zig), with N, the average length and each df counted over the visible files. Roots and labels narrow these statistics; a path prefix does not, as for the body (S3-T3).
   - coverage(f) = |{t ∈ Q : t in f's title}| / |Q|
   - title(f) = w · BM25_title(f, Q) · coverage(f), with **w = 2.5**.

   **Where the title part goes.** It is added to one chunk per file: the file's best body chunk by body score (ties to the smaller chunk id, bytewise), or its first chunk (smallest start line, end line, id) when no chunk of the file matches the body. Every other chunk keeps its body score.

   **Why w = 2.5.** The measured plateau: control 1.000 in both variants for w 1.0–5.0; title ≥ 0.909 for 2.0–5.0; every other slice flat for 2.0–3.0. 2.5 is the middle of that range, not the best point: 2.75 and above reach 1.000 only through `ti09`. The weight is a constant in one place on each surface. Re-tuning it after this task's run is not allowed; a new w needs a new pre-registered bar (the research doc's rule).

3. **Where it lives (Zig).** A new `zig/src/title_field.zig`:

   - **The derive.** It takes the opened generation's documents and roots and returns:
     - the file table: the first chunk and the token count per file;
     - `file_of` (chunk → file);
     - title postings indexed by file ordinal;
     - a sorted term table for lookup.

     Do not assume each file's chunks are adjacent. Use one pass when they are, and a fallback grouping when they are not; name it in a test.
   - **Memory.** The derive builds into one exact block, as S4-T9 does for the file-name tables, through a scratch allocator released before it returns. It does not build through `lexical_build.build` in an arena: that measured 14.8 MB transient at 10k.
   - **When it runs.** `scope.Folder.titleField()` derives it at the first content or `both` query of an opened generation, under the `Loads` lock, mirroring `filenameDecoded`. `ss_open` does not derive it.
   - **Every entry point applies it.** `ss_query`, `searchd query`, `serve`, the RPC service and the MCP server all go through the scoped service. A plain `Engine` query with no title field (tests, import paths) is today's ranking.

   **The `content` path.**
   - `Engine.queryTraced` takes the field through `SearchOptions` (for example `title: ?*const title_field.Field` and `title_weight: f32 = 2.5`).
   - It wraps `postings.SparseScorer` in a source that streams body matches in document order, one file at a time. It adds the title part to that file's best chunk, merges in the first chunks of title-only files in ordinal order, and answers `scoreOf` consistently.
   - `hybrid.searchSparseTraced` is unchanged: it takes any `Lexical`. Depth (`candidate_k`) cuts the lexical channel after the title part is added. With a vector channel, the title part is inside the lexical channel before RRF.

   **The `both` path.** `filename_field.queryDecoded`:
   - per file: the best body chunk's BM25 plus the title part;
   - one result per file, chosen by `BoundedTop(top_k)` over the merged scores, with ties by path, then chunk id (today's `hitLess` order);
   - the cited chunk is the best body chunk, or the file's first chunk;
   - the stem channel is still computed, for `matched_in`, `match_sections` and `ranking.filename` only, and no longer ranks;
   - `candidate_k` no longer cuts a channel before the merge (a cut before a sum is wrong). It is still validated (≥ `top_k`). The contract text says so.

   `filename` is unchanged. `both` still needs the file-name sections (`FilenameIndexUnavailable` unchanged).

4. **The Python reference's mirror** (`src/search_platform/core.py`).
   - A `_title_field(index, root_ids)` built per call, or cached by index identity, from the chunks' paths.
   - Root ids come from `index["roots"]` when the index has them (FolderIndex), else from a `title_root_ids` argument to `search`. `relevance_smoke.py` passes `judgments["roots"]` for `--pack j2` and for `--pack family-folder`, so a union index strips its roots as `searchd` does.
   - **`content`:** `search()` adds the title part to the lexical channel's chunk scores before ranking (lexical and hybrid).
   - **`both`:** `_search_filename_fields` replaces the RRF with the merge of ruling 3.
   - BM25 is f64 in sorted term order as today. Coverage counts distinct `_filename_tokenize` terms.

   The prototype (`docs/tasks/S5-T2-prototype.md`, `scripts/prototypes/s5t2/proto_s5t2.py`) is the reference for the expected numbers. Its candidate `C1-fs-best-w2.5` is this ruling.

5. **The contract** (proposed: contracts **2.2.0 → 2.3.0**, additive).
   - **`search_knowledge` result.** `retrieval.title_policy_id: "title-field-v1"` (const), present whenever the lexical channel ran under `content` or `both`.
   - **Each result.** An optional `ranking.title: {score: number|null}`: the weighted title part added to that result. It is null when none was added; under `content` it appears only on the one chunk per file that received it.
   - **`ranking.lexical.score`** is the lexical channel's score, now body plus title part. Rank and score stay consistent, and the body part alone is `lexical.score − title.score`.
   - **`both`.** The `search_field` description says `both` is the score-level merge and that `candidate_k` does not cut it.
   - **Unchanged:** no request field, no C ABI signature change, no index format change.
   - **The Dart binding.** It regenerates its contracts and bumps its version (see the open questions). `skills/folder-knowledge/SKILL.md` gets one sentence: a file's folder names and file name now count toward a `content` match, and `both` ranks a file by its best passage plus its name.

   *If the orchestrator rules for no contract change instead* (the title part folded silently into `lexical.score`): no Dart contract regeneration, the natives-only pin. That alternative is in the open questions.
